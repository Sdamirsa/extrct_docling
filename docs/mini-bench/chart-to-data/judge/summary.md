## Assessments

**gemma4_12b-it-q8_0.** The model is reliable only on a single-panel chart whose numbers are printed and large (B1: 12 of 12 counts exact) and gives usable eyeball estimates on a simple single-panel forest plot (B4: all centres within 2 % of the range); the third correct figure, C5, was read by the engine's vector pie reader without calling the model. Its typical failure is structural: every multi-panel data figure except C1 was merged into one panel or only partly read (B2, B3, B5, C3, C4), which loses whole panels (120 of 180 values in B2, 27 of 30 in B5, 16 of 24 in C4, all 264 in C3) and attaches values to the wrong panel, model name or axis. Where it places marks for the engine to measure, the marks are not on the data (C1: 9 of 12 bars read 0.04-0.24 too low because the marks sit inside the bars; A3: centres 0.02-0.04 low), and interval ends, whisker tops and outliers come back as extra plain values (A3, B5, C4). Small printed text is unsafe: 6 of 15 AUROC labels in B3 were misread and percent turned into fractions. On figures without data it declined three diagrams, leaving only empty panel shells, but invented a line series for ECG traces (A5, 102 rows, 632 s) and took a worked example's output as data (C2). Several of the scorer's zeros (A3, C1, C4) are naming or pairing mismatches that overstate the error, but the underlying extractions are still not usable as they are: trust it on single-panel charts with printed values, and not on multi-panel, measured or dense figures.

**gemma4_26b-a4b-it-q8_0.** The model is dependable on two things: copying numbers that are printed next to the marks of a single-panel chart (B1, 12 of 12 exact) and staying silent on diagrams (four of five no-data figures returned no values; the fifth, the ECG traces, got 16 empty rows). Whenever a value has to come from a mark position it is unreliable, in 6 of the 10 data charts: marks land inside bars or beside points (C1: 7 of 12 bars 0.09-0.24 low; A3: all three centres about 0.03 high), and where no axis was calibrated it falls back to rounded guesses that are roughly right but flatten differences (B4, B5, C4). Multi-panel figures are its second weakness: panels were merged or lost in four of them (B3 five ROC panels into one with a fabricated 0.0-1.0 ramp, C3 two of three panels missing, C4 box plot dropped, B5 two panels merged and mislabelled), and one three-panel line figure produced nothing at all (B2). Only 3 of 10 data charts are usable as returned, and one of those (C5) was read by the engine's vector path without a model call. It can be trusted on single-panel charts with printed values and as a non-chart filter; its measured or estimated values and anything with several panels need verification before use.

**gemma4_26b-a4b-it-qat.** The model is safe on figures without data: none of the five diagrams or traces produced a value, although one of them (A5) was simply never read because the time budget ran out before any model call. It reads printed numbers exactly: B1's 12 bar labels and B3's 15 AUROC values are all right, and C5's pies are right too, but those came from the engine's vector path with no model call. Its typical failure is layout: on multi-panel figures the panels are merged, half found or shifted (B3, B5, C3, C4, and B2 where nothing came back), which loses most of the data or attaches it to the wrong panel, and it habitually repeats the category as the series name, which makes the automatic scorer under-count correct numbers (A3, B5). Values that must be read from mark positions are unreliable: marks land beside the data (A3 centres at the lower cap, C1 with 8 of 12 bars wrong and two panels in reversed order), box-plot medians are eyeballed to within 1 to 7 % of the range, and interval bounds are missing, swapped or meaningless (B4, B5). Trust it on a single-panel chart whose values are printed in the figure; do not take measured values, intervals or anything from a multi-panel figure without checking. Of the 10 data charts only 3 are usable as returned.

**gemma4_31b-it-q8_0.** On single-panel figures the model is dependable: the grouped bar chart with printed counts (B1) is exact, and the two interval plots (A3, B4) are within 5 % of the axis range, although the centres carry a constant -0.02 offset in A3 and are rounded to 0.1 in B4. The three pies (C5) are exact too, but they were read by the engine's vector path without any model call. Every multi-panel data figure fails, and mostly by data that never comes back: B2 timed out at 600 s with no output, B3 returned 15 rows without a single value and ignored the printed AUROCs, B5 and C1 left 7 of 9 and 2 of 4 panels 'not read', and C3 found 2 of 22 items; panels are merged or lost in five figures. Where it does measure marks in such figures the numbers are unreliable (C1 bars 0.17 too low, C3 segments off by up to 20 points and one negative), and box-plot whiskers are guessed as symmetric bands. On non-charts it returns no numbers in four of five cases but always wraps the diagram in a chart-typed empty panel, and it turned an ECG trace into a 10-row line series. It can be trusted on a single-panel chart with printed values or a handful of clearly separated marks, and not on multi-panel, stacked or box-plot figures; a call takes 100-400 s, which is what exhausts the per-figure budget.

**gemma4_31b-it-qat.** The model is reliable on single-panel charts whose numbers are printed (B1 exact; the C5 pies are exact too, but the engine read those without calling the model) and gives usable approximate readings of one simple unlabelled panel (B4 forest plot, all centres within 1.7 % of the axis range). Its typical failure is structural: every multi-panel data figure lost panels, either merged under one axis (B2, B3, C4), merged or mislabelled (B5), reduced to a fragment (C3) or left 'not read' (6 panels of B5, panel 4 of C1), and that is where most of the 560 truth values went missing or became wrong. Where it does measure positions, the marks tend to land below the true mark (A3 centres 0.01-0.03 low, C1 bars 0.04-0.07 low), and where no axis is calibrated it returns rounded guesses with invented symmetric whiskers or bounds (B5, C4, B4). On the five no-data figures it never invents a number, but it never returns a clean empty result either: three get an empty 'stacked_bar' shell and two (A4, A5) get rows with null values, 76 of them on the ECG traces after 667 s. It also ignored printed numbers when they sat in legends of a multi-panel figure (B3: 15 AUROC values, none read). It can be trusted on a single-panel bar chart with printed labels and, to about two significant digits, on a single-panel interval plot; multi-panel figures, dense line or stacked-bar charts and box plots should not be taken from it as they are.
