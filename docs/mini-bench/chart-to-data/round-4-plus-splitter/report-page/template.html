Chart Splitter Check

Chart-to-data mini bench · round 4 plus · 19 November 2026 · 51 figures, 236 charts

Before any method reads a figure, the prepare phase cuts it into its charts. Round 4 found that cut wrong on 6 of the 11 chart figures of one paper. This check measures the cut alone, for twelve ways of making it, and asks what should decide it. A final test then put the design that came out of it against two additions: the figure's caption, and a model ruling between two cuts.

What the caption does to the chart list

The ruling call

Asked only where the two cuts differ. The model sees both drawn on the figure and answers with a number of charts and a boolean.

Every splitter on every figure

A column is one figure, with the number of charts it holds on top. A figure is cut right when the splitter returns that many charts and every box sits on its chart, overlapping it by half or more. Open a cell to see the boxes on the figure.

cut right ≈right number of charts, a box off its chart 6wrong number: the charts it returned

A box on the right chart is not yet a crop

A chart is found when a box overlaps it by half. It is whole when that box also holds nine tenths of the chart, tick labels and titles included: only then can a reader work from the crop. A model's own boxes clip. The same boxes, used as seeds of the rule splitter's cutter, come out whole.

charts foundof them, whole in their box

A second splitter as judge

Read as: a splitter's cut is kept when the judge finds the same number of charts, and the judge's cut is taken when it does not. No truth is used in that choice. Figures cut right, of 51.

Overruling costs the good splitters

It helps the two weak ones with FigEx2 as judge. It takes a quarter to a half of the figures from the cloud models, because the judge is then the weaker splitter of the two.

Agreement is the signal

Where the rules and FigEx2 find the same number of charts, the rules' cut is right on all 20 figures. Whenever a cloud model and a judge agree, the cloud model's cut is right.

So a detector's place is to flag doubt

"These two disagree, do not trust this cut" is something nothing in the step says today.

The model counts, the rules cut

A model's best answer is how many charts there are. Its weakest is where their edges are. The first table is the arbiter as it was measured: the model's count takes the rules' cut when it has that many charts, else Heron's, else the model's own boxes.

Scored afterwards, on the same 51 figures. The second table is one rule for every reading method, written after the tables above were read: the rules' cut when it has the model's count, else the model's boxes as seeds of the rules' cutter. It needs no detector. It is a reading of these results, not a test of them.

FigEx2

What it is

Two models that work as one (Song et al., EMNLP 2026). A captioning model, Qwen3-VL-8B with the authors' adapters, lists the panels of a figure by their printed letters. A detector with 26 classes, the letters A to Z, draws at most one box per letter. Trained on biomedical figures.

As a splitter

What it would cost to use

A 17.5 GB base model beside its own 2.6 GB, 8 seconds per figure, a second Python environment, and no licence stated in either repository. Most chart grids have no printed letters for its detector to go by.

Its captioning model as a value reader, beside the four of round 4

Asked once per figure, in plain words, for a table of values, on the six main figures of the showcase paper. Values right within 5 % of the axis under the right printed name, of 573. Its 285 overstates it: 140 are zeros and complements it wrote for bar segments it did not read, which the 5 % tolerance accepts.

rightFigEx2: estimated in round numbersFigEx2: filled in, not read

Not a run of the step: one question per figure, no prepared context, no crops, no checks. Its answers are cut at 2,048 tokens, about 90 rows, so Figures 4 and 5 are read in part.

Was FigEx2's code safe to run

Third-party code and weights, run on a machine that holds unpublished papers and API keys. A separate agent reviewed everything, reading only. Its verdict: safe with caveats, nothing malicious found. The review came after the first runs.

Code

The authors' two main files were read in full, and every changed part of their detector file against the library's own. No network call, no shell, no reading of the environment or of files outside the folders it is given.

Model files

All seven weight files are plain safetensors tables, a format that cannot carry code. No pickle file, no Python file. All 30 files hash to what the hub recorded.

Caveats, and what was done

The authors' loaders pass trust_remote_code=True, and one fallback could have asked the hub for a third repository. Our two wrapper scripts now take local folders only, give the fallback the same local folder, and switch the hub libraries to offline mode.

Not covered

The libraries themselves, the two repositories after the versions below, and any watching of the process while it runs. It runs as the user, without a sandbox. A new download needs a new review.

What this says for the step

The reading model lists the charts

One overview call, with the prompt the local engine already has. Sonnet has the number of charts right on all 51 figures, Gemini on 50, Gemma on 42. The rules have it right on 26.

The rules draw the edges

The splitter's own cutter, given one seed per chart, cuts along the widest ink-free band and trims each cell to its ink. That turns 134 whole charts into 220 for Gemini's boxes.

Agreement says how far to trust it

Where Gemma and the rules find the same number of charts, the rules' cut is right on all 22 figures. Where they differ, the record should say so.

Left out

FigEx2, for its size, its licence and its letters. Heron as a splitter: 10 of 11 on the showcase paper, 5 of 10 on the bench, 9 of 30 elsewhere. Neither adds a figure once a model's count is used.

None of this is built. The check changed no engine code.

Limits

The truth is drawn by a model

One run per model

Round 3 saw the same model return different counts for one figure in two runs. A difference of one or two figures between two rows is within that.

Medical-AI papers only

51 figures of 14 papers. The showcase paper's grey plot backgrounds are what makes Heron see its panels as pictures.

Gemini ran at low effort

At high effort it took 232 seconds and 9.8 cents for one single-chart figure. That run was stopped after six figures. Sonnet ran at high and barely thinks on this call.

FigEx2 off its ground

It was trained on lettered biomedical panels. The image size of 1,024 px and the score cut of 0.5 are our choices.

Spend

0.87 USD on OpenRouter: Sonnet 0.53, Gemini at low effort 0.16, the stopped Gemini run 0.18. Five agents: four for the truth, one for the safety review.
